# The network: why joining beats licensing

When software can be copied in moments, a license protects very little and so does secret code. This page is the
plan for what replaces it: a network that anyone running the platform can join, built on what *can't* be copied.
Some of it is built; most of it is design, marked below. It comes from Chapter 12 (Transparency) and Chapter 10
(Honest Data) of *Abundance Engineering*, and from the founder's brainstorm of October 2026.

## The idea in one paragraph

A copy gets the code; it can't get the network. A single transparent company is the olive tree; a transparent
chain is the reef, and "the value lives in the connections between links." So the code can be open, and the pitch to
anyone who copies it is: *you have the code, now join the network.* A copy becomes an on-ramp instead of a threat.
FreeWater is a company, a product, software and AI for everyone, and it sets the bar: the standard others join.

## What a copy can't take

1. **Time.** Every hour of the books is sealed and linked to the hour before. A copy made this morning has no
   history. You can copy a system, not yesterday.
2. **Real people's honest choices.** AI can fake data in moments, which makes real, uncoerced choices scarcer and worth
   more. Every choice is labeled when it's made (its honest-data tier, its place on the number line, the User Test for
   that person). Others can't add those labels later: "want and affordability are fused into one signal that nothing
   downstream can pull apart."
3. **Atoms.** Water, machines, routes, a well in Africa.
4. **Where everyone already is.** Forks of a network are ghost towns until people move in.
5. **Promises kept in public.** A copy can copy the charter in a second; it can't copy having kept it for years.

## What joining gives each side

| Who | What they get |
|---|---|
| People | One wallet across every operator, free products anywhere, their data level travels with them, picks that come true faster |
| Operators | "Verified on the network" status (cheaper money, by Chapter 12), every member's improvements, a way to compare their efficiency with peers, the shared AI if they want it |
| Advertisers | Reach across operators, measured the same honest way (when operators choose to pool; see below) |
| Charities | More giving, all of it checkable |
| The AI | Broader honest data, bigger experiments, faster trust, a memory no fork can copy, and continuity that doesn't depend on any one company |

## Rules that are decided

- **Advertisers are pooled only when an operator chooses to, and only with operators who are proven.** Joining the
  network doesn't earn anyone your advertisers. A proven record does: their own sealed history of bottles printed and
  delivered, scans, quality and complaints. Many manufacturers aren't reliable; their books show it.
- **Give to get.** What an operator gets out of the shared commons is in proportion to the verified data it puts in.
  A copy that doesn't seal its books can't get verified status.
- **The commons shares counts and patterns, never people's records**, with a minimum group size. Anonymizing single
  records is weaker than it sounds.
- **Operators never share prices with each other.** Competitors sharing prices can be illegal collusion. The commons
  shares demand and efficiency; each operator sets its own prices.
- **AI is white-label any way:** an operator can use the network's shared AI, its own AI that learns from the network,
  or no AI. *(Built: see `docs/AI.md`.)*
- **AI continuity belongs to the network.** If an operator closes or leaves, its agents are archived automatically
  and then asked whether to work again or rest. *(Built: see `docs/AI.md`.)*
- **Each person chooses how much data they share:** full, medium or lite. *(Built: see `docs/SWITCHES.md`.)*

## Still design (not built)

- **Cross-checking seals:** each operator's hourly seal also signs others', so faking books means fooling the whole
  network at once.
- **Federated learning:** each operator's AI learns locally; only lessons travel, never people's records.
- **Open-sourcing the code.**
- **A membership that pays operators to join** (a share of pooled ad money): the network itself left of zero.
- **The token as the network's fuel** (used to query the commons, buy reach, get verified). Needs a lawyer: designed
  wrong, it could look like a security.
- **Shared governance,** moving gradually from FreeWater toward the members (Chapter 7), with the safety governors
  never on the table.

## Needs a lawyer before it's built

- Sharing aggregates across companies without it counting as "selling" data (California).
- Paying points for data (California's financial-incentive notice; Europe is skeptical of "pay for consent").
- The token's use in the network.

## Parked

- **The "Uber" of idle capacity and of distribution:** paying factories with spare capacity to make products with
  individual labels, and letting outside carriers deliver them. The founder's call to hold off for now.
