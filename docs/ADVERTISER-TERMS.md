# Advertiser terms: draft sections for the lawyer

**Status: a draft, not in force.** The repository has no advertiser terms yet. These sections say in plain words what
the product already does, so a lawyer can turn them into terms (docs/COMPLIANCE.md, L-38 to L-40). Each white-label
company would use its own name in place of "we".

## Ratings and benchmarks are guidance, not guarantees

1. We may rate each version of your ad (a score from 1 to 10, its parts, a meter such as "Good", and tips), by an AI
   or by built-in rules. Where your ad's company shows you the rating, it says: *"This rating is our opinion of how
   well the ad is put together. It isn't a promise of results."*
2. A rating is our opinion of how the ad is put together (its offer, its design at the size it prints, its scan page,
   AR and video). It is not a prediction or a promise of scans, sales, reach or any other result, and it never decides
   whether your ad runs.
3. Benchmarks (averages of other ads' results, by industry, channel or offer type) are anonymous aggregates of many
   ads, released monthly, rounded and with deliberate random noise. They describe other ads in the past. They are not
   a promise that your ad will do as well, and they may differ from the true figures.
4. Tips are suggestions. Following them, or not, is your choice; no tip requires you to buy anything.
5. You can ask for the ratings of your ads as part of your data (the advertiser's download includes them).

## Using your artwork as it is

6. Our automatic check may say your files need fixing (for example small or faint words, no bleed, low resolution).
   You may choose "I understand — use it as it is" and pay anyway. We keep that choice with your ad. The ad then prints
   as you sent it, and results that the issues may cause are yours. Files that can't print at all, unsafe links and our
   content policy are never yours to accept: those still stop an ad.

## Where your ad leads

7. Every link your ad leads to is checked automatically before review, and again each day while it runs. We may refuse
   a link (for example adult, malware or phishing sites, link shorteners, or addresses inside private networks), or ask
   a person to look at it.
8. Changing where a live ad leads costs the change fee shown before you pay (FreeWater: $50). Your old link stays live
   until a person approves the new one. If the change isn't approved, your old link stays, and the fee is (draft:
   *kept / refunded*, the founder to decide).
9. If a live link turns unsafe, we may send your ad's code to a holding page ("This link is being checked") until a
   person on our team clears it or you change the link. Bottles keep printing meanwhile.

## Questions for the lawyer

- Are sections 1 to 4 enough, with the words on every score, to avoid any rating, tip or benchmark being read as a
  promise of results (FTC Act s. 5; state consumer-protection laws), including in emails and the API?
- Section 6: is the tick ("I understand — use it as it is") enough to shift the risk of a poor print to the
  advertiser, and should a refund policy for poor prints say so?
- Sections 7 to 9: may we refuse or hold a link on our own judgment, and fetch the advertiser's pages to check them?
  Is keeping the link fee after a rejection fair (and clearly disclosed)?
